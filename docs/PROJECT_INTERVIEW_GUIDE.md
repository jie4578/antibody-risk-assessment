# Project Interview Guide

## Why build this?

Antibody sequence analysis is a useful setting for combining deterministic
domain rules, reproducible experimental-property modeling, evidence retrieval,
and guarded AI explanation. The project demonstrates how to keep those layers
useful without presenting a research assistant as an autonomous scientist.

## Why start with rules?

Rules are inspectable, deterministic, and easy to test against known motif
definitions. They provide a transparent baseline before introducing a model
whose behavior and experimental validity are harder to interpret.

## Why validate instead of just adding ML?

Real experimental labels determine whether a model is learning useful signal
or merely reproducing a software label. The validation phases froze the rule
baseline, separated datasets, recorded provenance, and preserved negative
results before any deployment interpretation.

## Why was Jain largely null?

The broad current-rule feature set showed weak or null associations across the
Jain retrospective assays. That is evidence against broad claims, not a
reason to tune rules until the result looks positive.

## What did AIntibody reveal?

It showed assay-specific oxidation/HIC relationships and modest composite
discrimination. The result supports liability-screening research use, not a
universal developability predictor.

## Why control VH length?

Sequence length can correlate with both motif counts and assay behavior. The
robustness analysis therefore examined oxidation/HIC relationships after
controlling for VH length instead of treating the raw association as causal.

## Why ESM2?

ESM2 provides a fixed protein-language representation that can capture
sequence context beyond hand-designed motifs. The project freezes the model
name, revision, pooling, dimensions, and input contract for reproducibility.

## Why Ridge / Logistic Regression rather than XGBoost as the primary benchmark?

The frozen benchmark favors simple, regularized estimators that are stable on
a small, structured population and easy to audit. More flexible models can be
useful later, but they should not be chosen because they improve an observed
TEST result.

## Why freeze Train / Validation / Test?

Freezing the split prevents repeated development decisions from leaking into
the final evaluation. The TEST set was opened once under the preregistered
protocol.

## Why was TEST opened only once?

Repeatedly inspecting TEST results turns the set into development data and
inflates confidence. The project records the single evaluation and its
immutable marker.

## Why can the TEST set not be reused?

Its labels and outcomes are now observed. A future claim needs a new untouched
sequence-diverse or prospective experimental cohort.

## Why does high residual sequence similarity matter?

Many TEST sequences remain close to TRAIN. This means the frozen result does
not establish family-independent generalization even if the held-out metric is
strong within the tested sequence landscape.

## Why are RULE48 and ML kept separate?

Rule features encode predefined liability hypotheses, while ESM2 models
selected experimental properties. Keeping them separate makes the evidence
traceable and avoids inventing an unsupported combined score.

## Why are mutation ML deltas not called improvements?

The delta is only the arithmetic difference between two antibody-level model
outputs. It has not been independently validated as a single-mutation effect
size and must not be converted into a better/worse label.

## Why is Research Decision Summary deterministic?

The summary is an auditable state transformation over evidence already
generated by the application. It does not silently rerun rules, ML, literature,
or an LLM when a user asks for a summary.

## Why does Copilot have no scientific tools?

The Copilot is an explanation layer, not a second scientific engine. Restricting
it to the current summary reduces hidden recomputation, unsupported claims, and
confusion about the source of truth.

## Why exclude raw VH/VL from remote Copilot context?

The summary normally contains the evidence needed for explanation without
sending full sequences to a remote Provider. This is a privacy-minimizing
default; users remain responsible for their Provider and data policy.

## What would you do next with more data?

Collect a genuinely sequence-diverse or prospective experimental cohort,
pre-register its analysis, preserve assay protocol identity, and evaluate
generalization without changing the frozen baseline to fit outcomes.

## What are the project's strongest limitations?

The rule score is not experimentally calibrated; the benchmark is internal and
has residual sequence similarity; mutation deltas are not measured effects;
literature is contextual; and the system is not a clinical or wet-lab
replacement.
